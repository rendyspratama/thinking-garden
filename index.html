📘 How Thinking Garden teaches
  • Computational thinking (ScratchJr, KIBO "Coding as Another Language", CS Unplugged): Patterns, What First? and Code the Bunny practise sequencing, finding rules, planning steps and debugging. Studies with 4–6 year olds show these skills grow with short, playful practice.
  • Deductive reasoning: Animal Houses is a picture logic grid (a child's Sudoku) with one clear rule: one of each animal in every row, then in every column too. Your child learns to start where they can be sure and to explain it: "This row has a cat and a dog, so it must be the bunny." Picking something that could fit but isn't certain gets the message "we can't be sure yet", which separates guessing from knowing.
  • Philosophy for Children (P4C): Odd One Out always asks "why?", and Big Questions is a mini "community of inquiry" at home. There are no wrong answers, but reasons matter. Ask: "How do you know?" "Can you think of another reason?"
  • Concrete → pictorial → abstract (Singapore approach): pictures first, then pattern letters like A B A B, and sorting rules that become hidden "secret rules".
  • Resilience & growth mindset (Carol Dweck): Ollie praises strategies and persistence, never "smart". Mistakes are "not yet", bugs are normal, choosing 💪 Trickier is praised as brave, and the thinking tree grows extra when your child keeps going after a mistake.
  • Calm breath: after two misses Ollie offers a breathing break, because a calm body helps a thinking brain.
  • Best way to play: together, 10–15 minutes. Ask your child to explain their thinking out loud.
